# Pump Selection FAQ

## Q1: How to choose pump type?

Main parameters:
1. Medium properties (corrosive, viscosity, solids)
2. Flow and head requirements
3. Temperature and pressure range

**Quick guide**:
- Corrosive/explosive → Magnetic Pump
- Precise metering → Metering Pump
- Solids/high viscosity → Diaphragm Pump
- Clean water → Centrifugal Pump

## Q2: What's the difference between magnetic and centrifugal pumps?

- **Magnetic**: Static seal, no leakage; for corrosive, flammable, high-purity
- **Centrifugal**: Mechanical seal, slight leakage; for clean water, circulation

## Q3: What media are diaphragm pumps suitable for?

- Solids-containing slurry
- High viscosity fluids (colloids, polymers)
- Flammable/explosive media (pneumatic)
- Mining, ceramics, oilfields

## Q4: What's the accuracy of metering pumps?

- Standard: ±1%
- Precision: ±0.5%
- High precision: ±0.25%

Affected by temperature, pressure, viscosity.

## Q5: Common selection mistakes?

**Mistake 1: Price only**  
Consider total lifecycle cost.

**Mistake 2: Oversized**  
Wastes energy, increases cavitation risk.

**Mistake 3: Ignoring medium**  
Concentration/temperature exceeding limits causes failures.

## Q6: Can magnetic pumps handle solids?

**Not recommended**. The containment shell can be worn by particles. Clean media only.

## Q7: How to determine pump head?

Head = Actual head + Pipe friction + Valve losses + Safety margin

Recommended: 10-20% safety margin.

## Q8: Why actual head is lower than rated?

Possible causes:
1. Inaccurate pipe friction calculation
2. Medium density differs from water
3. Pump speed lower than rated
4. Cavitation at inlet

## Q10: What parameters are needed for selection?

- Medium name and chemical composition
- Concentration, temperature
- Flow requirements (normal/max)
- Head requirements
- Inlet pressure conditions
- Site environment (explosion-proof, cleanliness)
- Connection type (flange/thread)
